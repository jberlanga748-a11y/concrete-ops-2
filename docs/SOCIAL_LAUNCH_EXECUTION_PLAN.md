# Apex HQ Social Launch Execution Plan

Status: 14-day launch plan
Use for: launching Apex HQ social profiles and building early demo conversations

## Purpose

Launch Apex HQ socially with a practical, trust-first strategy focused on local contractors, GCs, remodelers, subcontractors, and construction business owners.

The goal is not to go viral. The goal is to create credibility, start conversations, collect feedback, book demos, and make Apex HQ feel real before the first demo push.

## Primary Social Priorities

### 1. LinkedIn

Best for:

- GCs
- construction business owners
- remodelers
- commercial/residential operators
- referral partners
- professional credibility

Use LinkedIn as the main trust-building channel.

### 2. Facebook

Best for:

- local contractor groups
- trade communities
- owner/operator discussions
- warm relationship building
- local discovery

Use Facebook for careful group engagement and community listening.

### 3. YouTube

Best for:

- hosting the demo video
- searchable product proof
- sharing a clean demo link
- future walkthroughs

Use YouTube as the demo content hub, not the daily posting priority at first.

## Daily Time Blocks For John

Morning, 20 minutes:

- check LinkedIn notifications
- reply to comments
- send 2-3 thoughtful connection requests
- comment on 3 contractor/business posts

Midday, 20-30 minutes:

- post or prepare the day's content
- engage in 1-2 Facebook groups
- save questions/pain points contractors mention

Evening, 20-40 minutes:

- follow up with warm conversations
- send demo link only when relevant
- track metrics
- write down objections, feature requests, and exact contractor language

## Local Contractor Group Rules

For the first 7 days, do not post direct promotions in groups unless clearly allowed.

Instead:

- comment on posts where contractors discuss estimating, scheduling, admin, photos, crews, or proposals
- ask useful questions
- share lessons learned
- mention Apex HQ only when context makes it natural

Good comment:

```text
We see the same thing with contractors: the estimate itself is only half the battle. The bigger issue is usually keeping notes, scope, pricing, and handoff details connected after the bid is accepted.
```

When it is appropriate:

```text
I am working on Apex HQ, which is built around this kind of contractor workflow. Happy to share the demo if useful, but I do not want to hijack the thread.
```

## 14-Day Plan

### Day 1: Set Foundation

Create or update:

- LinkedIn Company Page
- John's LinkedIn profile
- Facebook Business Page
- YouTube Channel

John LinkedIn post:

```text
I am building Apex HQ for contractors who want a cleaner way to manage leads, estimates, jobs, crews, photos, reports, and follow-ups.

The goal is simple: reduce the mess between rough notes, customer communication, job details, and the information crews need once the work is won.
```

### Day 2: Define The Problem

Post:

```text
A lot of construction software feels like it was designed after the job is already organized.

But for many contractors, the messy part happens earlier:

- notes from a walkthrough
- photos and scope details
- pricing decisions
- customer follow-up
- schedule and crew handoff
- field proof after the work starts

Apex HQ is being built around that real workflow.
```

Facebook group activity:

```text
For contractors here: what part of the estimate-to-job process eats the most time?
```

### Day 3: Introduce The Workflow

Company page post:

```text
Apex HQ focuses on the path from lead to estimate, job setup, field handoff, photo/report proof, and follow-up. The product is being shaped around contractor workflows where details can easily get lost.
```

## 14-Day Success Targets

A good first 14 days:

- 100-250 relevant LinkedIn connections added
- 10-25 meaningful contractor conversations
- 3-8 feedback/demo calls booked
- 100+ demo video views from relevant audiences
- 5+ clear product objections captured
- 5+ reusable phrases from contractors
- 2-3 local groups identified as worth continued participation

Do not judge success by likes alone. Conversations and feedback matter more.

## Risks

Risk: sounding too promotional.
Mitigation: ask for feedback before asking for demos.

Risk: posting too broadly.
Mitigation: speak directly to contractors, GCs, remodelers, and construction business owners.

Risk: demo video feels too long.
Mitigation: use short clips for social and full YouTube video for serious prospects.

Risk: feedback gets lost.
Mitigation: log every useful comment, objection, and phrase daily.
