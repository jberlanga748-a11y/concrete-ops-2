- Would this help you or your field team know what needs done without chasing texts?
- Would having photos and reports tied to the job save time later?

- What part made the most sense?
- What part felt confusing or too much?
- Where would this actually save time?
- What would you use first: leads, estimates, jobs, field updates, photos/reports, or follow-up?
- If we tested this for 14 days, what real workflow should we test?
- Would you pay to keep that workflow if it worked?
- Who else should see it if the one workflow helps?

## Product Issues Learned From Prospects

- Solo contractors may only need the Basic path; too much crew/operations depth can make the first demo feel heavy.
- Website users may assume Apex HQ replaces Wix instead of organizing the workflow after lead intake.
- Fence targets likely need a simple lead/estimate/job/photo/follow-up story, not a concrete-only pitch.
- Crewed contractors care most about field handoff, proof photos, reports, and owner review, but field adoption is still a pilot risk.
- Prospects asking for guaranteed jobs need careful qualification; Apex HQ should not be sold as a lead-generation guarantee.
- Contractors with accounting/payroll urgency may be poor first pilots unless they can test a non-accounting workflow first.
- Setup burden is a major pilot risk; the first 14 days must start from one real estimate, job, or field action.
- Business email/sender handling remains a sales-readiness risk; cold email should not go broad until John manually confirms footer/address/compliance handling.
- Inbox reliability remains a customer-success risk because email triage has flagged Gmail storage pressure.
- Apex OS/private operator work may distract demos; customer walkthroughs should stay on Apex HQ contractor workflows, not private operator features.
