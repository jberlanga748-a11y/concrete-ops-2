# Apex HQ Founder Pay And Reinvestment Plan

Status: planning draft, not tax/legal advice
Founder: John Berlanga
Use for: deciding when to pay yourself, what to save for taxes, and what to reinvest into Apex HQ

## Important Caution

This is a business planning system, not CPA, legal, or tax advice.

Before meaningful revenue starts, talk to a CPA or tax pro. The point of this plan is to keep Apex HQ from starving while also not pretending John can work forever without pay.

## Current Stage

Apex HQ is in founder-led demo and controlled pilot stage.

That means the company needs cash discipline more than fancy finance.

Current priorities:

1. Keep the app alive.
2. Get first demos.
3. Convert first paid pilots.
4. Avoid tax surprises.
5. Reinvest into the pieces that create revenue.
6. Pay John modestly once the business can handle it.

## Official Tax Facts To Respect

Based on IRS small business guidance:

- self-employed people generally need to plan for income tax and self-employment tax
- estimated tax payments may be needed when tax is not withheld
- business records must be kept
- startup and business expenses should be tracked clearly

Sources:

- IRS Publication 334, Tax Guide for Small Business
- IRS Publication 583, Starting a Business and Keeping Records
- IRS self-employment tax guidance
- IRS Publication 505, Tax Withholding and Estimated Tax
- SBA startup cost guidance

## Simple Money Buckets

Every dollar Apex HQ brings in should be split into buckets before spending.

### Bucket 1: Taxes

Purpose:

- avoid surprise tax bills
- prepare for estimated payments

Starting rule:

```text
Set aside 25% to 30% of net business income for taxes until a CPA gives better guidance.
```

Do not spend this money.

### Bucket 2: Core Operating Costs

Purpose:

- keep Apex HQ running

Examples:

- domain/email
- hosting/infrastructure
- AI/API usage
- software tools
- basic support tools
- business phone/email tools

Rule:

```text
Core operating costs get paid before founder pay.
```

### Bucket 3: Reinvestment

Purpose:

- turn revenue into more demos, better onboarding, better support, and proof

Use for:

- better demo/video tools
- outreach tools
- CRM/tracker tools if needed
- contractor data verification
- sales collateral
- customer onboarding assets
- support documentation
- CPA/legal setup when cash allows

Rule:

```text
Reinvest first into things that create qualified conversations, pilots, retention, or proof.
```

### Bucket 4: Founder Pay

Purpose:

- keep John alive and focused without draining the company too early

Rule:

```text
Founder pay starts small and grows only when revenue is recurring and the business has reserves.
```

### Bucket 5: Emergency Reserve

Purpose:

- protect the business from panic decisions

Rule:

```text
Build toward 1 month of core operating costs first, then 3 months.
```

## Stage-Based Pay Plan

### Stage 0: Pre-Revenue / No Reliable Revenue

Revenue:

- $0 to first paid pilot

Founder pay:

- $0 from Apex HQ unless absolutely necessary

Reinvestment:

- only essentials

Spend allowed:

- domain/email
- hosting/app essentials
- minimal tools
- demo/recording essentials if needed

Do not spend on:

- paid ads
- expensive software stacks
- agencies
- broad automation
- swag
- unnecessary subscriptions

Goal:

```text
Get to first paid pilot without creating monthly bills the business cannot support.
```

### Stage 1: First Paid Pilots

Revenue:

- $99 to $1,000 MRR

Founder pay:

- optional small draw only after taxes and core costs are reserved
- default: reinvest most revenue

Suggested split:

| Bucket | Percent |
| --- | ---: |
| Tax reserve | 25%-30% |
| Core operating costs | actual cost |
| Reinvestment | 50%-70% of what remains |
| Founder pay | 0%-20% of what remains |
| Emergency reserve | anything left |

Best use of reinvestment:

- demo video
- sales sheet
- better onboarding docs
- prospect research
- support cleanup

Goal:

```text
Prove people will pay and that support does not crush John.
```

### Stage 2: Early MRR

Revenue:

- $1,000 to $5,000 MRR

Founder pay:

- small consistent owner draw if cash flow is stable
- do not exceed what the company can repeat monthly

Suggested split after tax reserve and core costs:

| Bucket | Percent |
| --- | ---: |
| Reinvestment | 50% |
| Founder pay | 25% |
| Emergency reserve | 25% |

Best use of reinvestment:

- onboarding improvements
- customer support process
- demo assets
- better sales tracking
- CPA/bookkeeping setup
- legal/business formation when cash allows

Goal:

```text
Build repeatable demos and pilots without starving the founder.
```

### Stage 3: Real Traction

Revenue:

- $5,000 to $10,000 MRR

Founder pay:

- consistent monthly draw
- keep it modest until churn and support load are understood

Suggested split after tax reserve and core costs:

| Bucket | Percent |
| --- | ---: |
| Reinvestment | 40% |
| Founder pay | 35% |
| Emergency reserve | 25% |

Best use of reinvestment:

- help with support/admin
- better product onboarding
- contractor case studies
- referral system
- careful automation
- CPA/legal setup if not already done

Goal:

```text
Make Apex HQ reliable enough that revenue can compound.
```

### Stage 4: Growth Mode

Revenue:

- $10,000+ MRR

Founder pay:

- formalize owner compensation with CPA guidance
- consider entity/tax structure review
- protect cash reserves

Suggested split after tax reserve and core costs:

| Bucket | Percent |
| --- | ---: |
| Reinvestment | 35%-45% |
| Founder pay | 30%-40% |
| Emergency reserve / hiring | 20%-30% |

Best use of reinvestment:

- support/sales help
- better onboarding
- customer success
- product reliability
- proof/case studies
- selective paid tests only after manual sales works

Goal:

```text
Scale without breaking support, trust, or cash.
```

## Founder Pay Rules

### Rule 1: Do Not Pay Yourself From Tax Money

Tax reserve is not founder pay.

### Rule 2: Do Not Pay Yourself From Money Needed To Keep The App Alive

Hosting, email, AI/API costs, and basic operating tools come first.

### Rule 3: Founder Pay Should Follow Recurring Revenue

Do not raise pay because of one setup fee.

Setup fees can help cash, but they are not the same as stable MRR.

### Rule 4: Raise Pay Only After 2 Stable Months

Before increasing founder pay, Apex HQ should have:

- stable MRR for 2 months
- no unpaid tax reserve gap
- core costs covered
- at least 1 month operating reserve started
- no urgent support fires requiring paid help

### Rule 5: Owner Draw Should Be Recorded

Track every founder draw:

```text
Date:
Amount:
Reason:
Revenue that month:
Tax reserve set aside:
Operating reserve after draw:
```

## Reinvestment Priority

Spend in this order:

1. Keep the app/email/domain alive.
2. CPA/bookkeeping basics once revenue starts.
3. Demo cleanup and demo video.
4. Outreach and sales tracking.
5. Customer onboarding/support docs.
6. Proof capture and case studies.
7. Contractor referral system.
8. Help with support/sales when founder is overloaded.
9. Selective paid marketing only after manual sales converts.

## Do Not Reinvest Into Yet

Avoid:

- paid ads before demo-to-pilot conversion is proven
- expensive sales tools before manual outreach works
- swag
- broad social production
- large contractor conferences
- custom features for one prospect
- unnecessary software subscriptions
- automation that sends messages without approval

## Setup Fees

Setup fees should not be treated like regular monthly income.

Use setup fees for:

- onboarding time
- templates
- training
- support setup
- customer success materials
- reserves

Suggested setup fee direction:

- Basic: $0-$500 depending on help needed
- Premium: $500-$1,500
- Elite: $2,500+

Founder pilot setup can be discounted, but do not train customers to expect unlimited help for free.

## Simple Starting Recommendation

Until Apex HQ reaches at least $1,000 MRR:

- do not take regular pay
- set aside 25%-30% for taxes
- keep core costs paid
- reinvest most remaining money into demo, outreach, onboarding, and proof

At $1,000-$5,000 MRR:

- allow a small consistent draw only if tax reserve and operating costs are covered
- still reinvest heavily

At $5,000-$10,000 MRR:

- pay John more consistently
- start building business reserve and support capacity

At $10,000+ MRR:

- formalize pay strategy with CPA guidance
- keep reinvestment high enough to keep growth moving

## Biggest Risk

The biggest risk is paying John too early from unstable money, then starving the company before it gets proof.

The second biggest risk is never paying John, causing burnout.

The answer is not one extreme.

The answer is staged pay:

```text
tax reserve -> core costs -> growth reinvestment -> modest founder pay -> operating reserve
```
